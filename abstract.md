
**The Role of Kaiso in Quadruple-Negative Breast Cancer.**

Triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) is an aggressive subtype defined by the absence of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2, leaving patients with limited targeted therapies and poor clinical outcomes. Notably, TNBC disproportionately affects women of African ancestry (WAA), who experience higher mortality rates compared to white women, despite lower incidence rates, suggesting an ancestral genetic susceptibility.&nbsp;

Our lab identified the transcription factor Kaiso as a potential driver of increased metastasis and mortality in WAA TNBC patients. Notably, 60–80% of TNBC cases also exhibit low androgen receptor (AR) expression, correlating with poor response to AR inhibitors and reduced overall survival. This AR-low phenotype defines a distinct TNBC subtype—quadruple-negative breast cancer (QNBC).

In silico analyses revealed multiple Kaiso binding sites within the AR promoter, suggesting that Kaiso may regulate AR expression. My research investigates how Kaiso influences AR expression and how this relationship may contribute to the increased aggressiveness and mortality observed in WAA with TNBC. This work provides mechanistic insight into ancestry-associated disparities in TNBC and highlights Kaiso as a promising therapeutic target.
